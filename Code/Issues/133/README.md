# #133: burn-in of negative density dependence

How many generations negative density dependence needs before it acts on mortality, and how `traitSpeciationJump` (#114), `traitEvolution` (#220) and the kernel change this.

## Files

- `burnIn.R`: simulations and summary (setting, seeds and versions in its header).
- `summary.csv`: means over 10 seeds per setting and generation (committed).
- `results/`: one table per run, regenerated by `burnIn.R` (not committed, see `.gitignore`).

## Setting

50 x 50 cells, `dispersal = 1`, `negativeDDCut = 2`, `specRate = 1`, other arguments at their defaults (`traitEvolution = 0.01`, `traitPull = 0.2`, `fitnessBaseMortalityRatio = 10`); 6 consecutive censuses from generation g, 10 seeds per setting. The measure of conspecific density dependence is the slope of a logistic regression of death within one generation on the number of conspecifics among the 12 neighbors (`getMortalityData()`). The standard deviation of the slope across seeds was at most 0.028.

## Conclusions

- With the defaults and the absolute kernel, density dependence on mortality reached 40-50% of its final strength at 3,000 generations and its final strength between 3,000 and 10,000 generations, for `negativeDD = 1` and 0.5. The competition trait splits into two groups at 0 and 1 (SD 0.48) by then.
- A jump of the competition trait at speciation shortens this: with `traitSpeciationJump = c(0, 0.3, 0)`, about 70% of the final strength after 300 and nearly all after 1,000 generations; with 0.1, about 3,000 generations. Faster trait evolution, `traitEvolution = c(0.01, 0.05, 0.01)`, gave a similar shortening (about 75% after 300 generations, all after 1,000).
- The Gaussian kernel (width 0.1) develops faster than the absolute kernel (final strength by about 3,000 generations without a jump, by 100 with a jump of 0.3).
- The species kernel acts fully from generation 100 on, as expected.
- The final strength differs between settings (e.g. 0.08 without and 0.12 with a jump of 0.3), so the shortcuts do not reach the same end state as the default; with the absolute kernel and a jump, richness was lower (7-9 instead of about 15 species).

Not checked: other grid sizes, dispersal distances and speciation rates; `fitnessActsOn` other than mortality; whether phylogenetic indicators reach equilibrium (they can still change after 50,000 generations, see the comments on #133).
